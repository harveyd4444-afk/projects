01 — The problem

AI replies are only as good as what they’re given.

A model left to answer support emails on its own will invent refund windows and delivery times that sound right but aren’t company policy. Pasting the whole policy handbook into every prompt is slow, costly and gets less reliable as the handbook grows.

02 — The solution

Retrieve the right policy, then generate.

  • →Company policy is chunked and stored as Gemini embeddings in a Postgres pgvector table
  • →Each incoming email is embedded with the same model and matched by semantic similarity
  • →The closest policy extract is injected into the answering agents’ system prompts
  • →Account-specific queries combine that extract with the customer’s SQL record
  • →Agents are told to follow the extract exactly and admit gaps rather than guess
03 — The RAG layer

How an email finds its policy.

Retrieval happens once, up front, so both answering agents share the same grounding.

Index

Policy → vectors.

Policy documents are split into extracts and embedded into the policy_docs2 pgvector table.

Embed

Email → vector.

The email body is embedded with Gemini, the same model used for the index, so both sit in one vector space.

Retrieve

Nearest match, top-k = 1.

A similarity search returns the single most relevant policy extract — matched on meaning, not keywords.

Augment

Extract → prompt.

The extract is injected into each answering agent’s system message as the only policy it may cite.

06 — Guardrails

What the agents are told.

  1. 01Use only the policy extracts provided; ignore any that aren’t relevant.
  2. 02If the extract doesn’t cover it, say so and promise a team follow-up.
  3. 03Where policy says escalate — e.g. suspected fraud — pass it on, don’t resolve.
  4. 04Never ask for or repeat full card numbers or passwords.
Key insight

Retrieval keeps the model honest. Drafts keep a human in charge.

Because each reply is built from a retrieved policy extract rather than the model’s memory, updating policy means updating the vector store — not rewriting prompts. And because every reply lands as a draft, the team still approves what customers receive.

07 — Design choices

Built to stay accurate.

  1. 01Retrieve once, upstream — both answering agents share one grounding, and the router never pays for it twice.
  2. 02Top-k of 1 keeps prompts short and focused on the single most relevant policy.
  3. 03Vectors and customer data both live in Postgres — one database, two kinds of search.
  4. 04Right-sized models — Flash-Lite for policy answers, Flash for routing and personalised replies.
